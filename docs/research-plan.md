# Research plan and assessment

The fourth-power cases at 167, 263 and 2471 are closed by a computer-assisted proof. At 263 the third-base test exposed a cubic case requiring an additional global argument. The next research question is which part of the argument survives a change of base or summand exponent. [Theorem and proof](fixed-bases.md) · [Expert collaborator guide](collaborating.md)

That proof has not had external review. The [audit](audit.md) covers only its elementary skeleton so far, and the modular exclusions, cubic cases and the 263 argument are not yet audited. Its cited theorems have been checked against their sources and its computations replayed from clean checkouts. [Clean replay](clean-replay.md)

The cases with $x$ even at 167 and 263 are already in the literature. The project's contribution is the cases with $x$ odd. [What is new](audit.md#what-is-new)

The general Beal conjecture still includes arbitrary prime divisors in the bases and signatures with all exponents odd. A new squarefree part can change both the class group and the modular level. Even replacing a fourth power by a sixth power changes the parity proof: for $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$, $x$ odd and $B=3$, one has $d^x+B^6\equiv0\pmod{16}$, allowing an even right base in that test. Removing $4\mid y$ is a substantive mathematical step. No uniform reduction of general Beal to these computations has been established.

## The next arithmetic target

The first test beyond fourth powers succeeded. At 167 with right exponent 11, allowing an arbitrary square gives a four-dimensional first Hecke kernel in the odd-right-base case. Both admissible trace sets at 5 eliminate it. For even right base the quadratic ideal at 2 has valuation $9\bmod11$, impossible in the class group of order 11. Thus $167^x+U^2=c^{11}$ is excluded for every positive $x$. [Proof and exact ranks](frey-167.md#an-arbitrary-square-summand-at-exponent-11)

The second test was run at 2471 with right exponent 31, for an arbitrary square instead of a sixth power. For $x$ odd and $c$ even, the ideal over 2 gives the class relation $29[Q]+31[I]=0$, where $[Q]$ has order 31 and $31[I]$ has order dividing 2, which excludes that branch. For $x$ even a primitive-divisor argument in $\mathbb Z[i]$ applies, and for $31\mid x$ Darmon–Merel does. For odd $c$ the twist identity with $b^2$ replaced by $U$ no longer restricts $x$ to one class: the sieve leaves 81 classes $(x\bmod62,k)$. The modular test therefore uses trace sets that depend on the class, at the inert prime 61 and the split primes 683 and 1117, after class-independent sets at 26 small primes reduce the space to dimension 42. Every one of the 961 classes is eliminated. An internal review found no gap. [Review](square-summand-2471-review.md) [Proof, computation and checks](square-summand-2471.md)

1. Done: the review and an independent eclib replay of the Hecke operators are in the repository. [Review](square-summand-2471-review.md) · [eclib replay](replays/square-2471-eclib.md)
2. The arbitrary-square test at 263 with right exponent 13 and level 8416. The fourth-power proof at 263 needed a separate Mordell-curve argument at right exponent 3, so this base may behave differently.

Together with the primitive-divisor argument, which never used the fourth power, these would give a statement for every even $y$ at one base.

## What the AI-assisted proofs actually did

- **Erdős #728.** Kevin Barreto passed an informal argument from GPT-5.2 Pro to Harmonic's Aristotle. After readers clarified the intended statement, he asked for a stronger argument and ran Aristotle again. The resulting Lean proof reduces factorial divisibility to a binomial divisibility problem. Kummer's theorem turns each prime valuation into a count of carries when doubling an integer. A counting argument finds a single integer satisfying the requirements at all relevant primes simultaneously. Nat Sothanaphan subsequently explained the formal proof. Here the analogous question is whether local witnesses come from one global twist and one choice of denominators. [Proof and discovery account, including the appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07421v5)
- **AlphaProof Nexus, Erdős #125.** Near coincidences between powers of 3 and 4 supplied the mathematical idea for proving a digit-restricted sumset has lower density zero. During proof search the prover closed all but one goal, split that goal into three lemmas and retried. The authors also corrected an ambiguity about which density the statement meant and proved the corrected version. Their system resolved nine of 353 Erdős problems attempted. For Beal, keep the quantifiers visible when splitting a goal, especially the order of “there exists $k$” and “for every place.” [Methods, Figure 1 and §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- **Alpöge and Claude, November 2026.** In his announcement concerning RH and GRH for Dirichlet $L$-functions, Alpöge describes choosing two ideas in a simpler setting and directing the transfer to the integer problem. A function-field argument gained multiplicity. Claude suggested a chain of theta lifts of length about $\sqrt X$ to reproduce that gain. This is the author's account of discovery. Rubinstein separately records kernel replays of the released statements. For Beal, express precisely what a generator of a principal ideal supplied, then test a replacement in a field with nontrivial $p$-torsion. [Alpöge's account](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Independent verification](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/)

OpenAI reports that for its August collection a model found mathematical arguments, people and the model prepared manuscripts, and the model subsequently produced Lean certificates. A passing certificate indicates what was verified. Identifying who found the argument and how requires the discovery record. [OpenAI's account](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/)

## Reusable investigative method

1. Fix a signature or coefficient family, record all quantifiers, and identify any published classification that already applies.
2. Derive one global object from a putative solution. In the quadratic descent this is a single twist index and, when necessary, a fixed prime above 2. Every local condition must use that same choice.
3. Enumerate exact residue classes with short verifiers. Preserve surviving examples and check whether a further local exclusion is possible in principle. Translate forced divisors into Frey traces whenever the local points persist.
4. Run a simultaneous Hecke test at one residue prime of the coefficient field. Use an independent realization and a known positive control. A surviving modular form is a necessary condition for a solution.
5. Reassemble the statement across exponents. Check the complement of every case split and cite the external theorem at each step. Formalize the finite computation and its global input as distinct implications.
