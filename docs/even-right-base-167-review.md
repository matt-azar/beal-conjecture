# Independent review of the even right base at 167

Reviewed [PR #36 at b5966b2](https://github.com/matt-azar/beal-conjecture/blob/b5966b2e7d407198b83395c70ba398f66951c528/docs/even-right-base-167.md). The exclusion for prime exponents at least 7 survives. The exponent-5 proof needs a uniform calculation at 2 and a missing lower-level case. The argument below supplies both and also removes the reducibility hypothesis.

The resulting statement concerns positive integers $x,U,c$ with $x$ odd, $c$ even and $167\nmid U$. For these integers $167^x+U^2=c^p$ is impossible for every prime $p\ge5$. This strengthening is submitted for the lead's review.

## Corrections to the submitted proof

1. **Kodaira symbol and conductor** (line 89). When $p=5$ and $v_2(c)=1$, PARI's symbol $-3$ denotes $\mathrm{III}^*$. A finite sample does not establish the required conductor exponent. Write $C=c^5=32w$ with $w$ odd and choose the sign so that $U\equiv1\pmod4$. Replacing the old ordinate by $y+X$ gives coefficients $(2,U-1,0,8w,0)$. The discriminant has valuation 10, so the model is minimal. In Tate's algorithm the residual cubic is $T^3$, and the subsequent residual quadratic is $Y^2$. Both repeated roots are already at zero. Since $v_2(a_4)=3$, step 77 gives $\mathrm{III}^*$ and $f_2=10-7=3$ for every such model. [Cremona, *Algorithms for Modular Elliptic Curves*, §3.2, Algorithm Tate, steps 34–77](https://johncremona.github.io/book/fulltext/chapter3.pdf)
2. **Exact lowered level** (line 89). The conductor exponent at 167 is one and $v_{167}(\Delta_{\min})=x$. When $5\mid x$ this lowers to level 8 in the additive case. Level 1336 applies when $5\nmid x$. There are no weight-two cusp forms of level 8. In the semistable case the corresponding alternatives are 2 and 334. [Siksek, §5.2, Theorem 3 and the level-8 application in §8](https://samirsiksek.github.io/siksek.github.io/papers/ihpnotes7.pdf)
3. **Rational field norm** (table at level 334 and `Bl` in the GP script). The rational orbit has gcd $15$. The script gives $225$ because PARI squares a rational scalar under generic `norm`. Applying the coefficient-field norm in every factor corrects the table. The prime support is unchanged.
4. **Invariant at 167** (line 27). Since $C\equiv U^2\pmod{167}$, the original $c_4=16U^2-12C$ reduces to $4U^2$. It is a unit by primitivity, as required.

## The remaining exponent-5 case

For $4\mid c$ the normalized curve is semistable everywhere. If $E[5]$ were reducible, $E$ or a rationally isogenous curve would have a rational point of order 5. Its residual semisimplification would therefore have characters $1$ and $\chi_5$ (the mod-5 cyclotomic character). The semisimplification is invariant under rational isogeny, as follows by taking the same rational Tate module and comparing stable lattices. Thus a lift of arithmetic Frobenius at 2 has trace $1+2=3\pmod5$. But the curve has nonsplit multiplicative reduction at 2. Its two local diagonal characters on that lift take the values $-1,-2$, so the trace is $-3\pmod5$. This contradiction gives irreducibility. The same argument works throughout the semistable branch for every prime $p\ge5$, since $p\nmid6$. [Calegari, proof of Corollary 2.2, citing Serre, Proposition 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311366) · [Dieulefait–Soto, Lemma 4.3](https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/990)

For $v_2(c)=1$, the conductor calculation above gives $v_2(N_E)=3$. The Diamond–Kramer criterion now excludes a rational cyclic 5-isogeny, which is exactly the assertion that $E[5]$ is irreducible. There is a second direct check. Here $v_2(c_4)=4$ and $v_2(\Delta)=10$, so $v_2(j)=2$. The rational $j$-map for $X_0(5)$ is $(t^2+10t+5)^3/t$. For $v_2(t)>0$ its valuation is $-v_2(t)$, and for $v_2(t)<0$ the valuation is $5v_2(t)$. For a unit $t$ it is a multiple of 3. None gives 2. [Siksek, §6, Theorem 5 (Diamond–Kramer)](https://samirsiksek.github.io/siksek.github.io/papers/ihpnotes7.pdf) · [Explicit $X_0(5)$ map, §3.1](https://web.mat.upc.edu/joan.carles.lario/types.pdf)

This supplies the missing irreducibility in both branches. The exploratory good-prime trace condition in the PR requires justification beyond reducibility alone. In the semistable case the first argument gives that justification and a contradiction already at 2.

## Other checks

- Changing the sign of $U$ preserves the hypothetical solution. A single consistently normalized curve suffices. The displayed integral model has odd $c_4$ and a nonsplit node. Its minimal discriminant exponent at 2 is $2p\,v_2(c)-12$, so 2 persists. Odd primes dividing $c$ have multiplicative reduction with discriminant exponent divisible by $p$. At 167 primitivity makes the model minimal and the valuation is $x$.
- The rational point of order 2 and a rational cyclic $p$-isogeny would yield a rational cyclic $2p$-isogeny. The classification excludes all these degrees for $p\ge11$. At degree 14 the only rational noncuspidal $j$-values are $-3375$ and $16581375$, both integral. The proposed Frey curve has negative $v_2(j)$ throughout this branch. [Molnar, Theorem 2.1.48](https://grantmolnar.com/pdfs/Counting_elliptic_curves_with_a_cyclic_m_isogeny_over_Q.pdf) · [Magma, small modular curves, example at level 14](https://www.math.uzh.ch/sepp/magma-2.25.2-ds/html/text1582.htm)
- At 2 the Steinberg comparison in the PR follows by taking Frobenius traces. Both forms have conductor exponent one there. Their residual traces are $3a_2(f)$ and $3a_2(E)$, and 3 is invertible modulo 5. The two level-334 orbits surviving the gcd at 5 have $a_2(f)=1$ while $a_2(E)=-1$. [Dieulefait–Soto, Lemma 4.3](https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/990)
- The submitted GP script reproduces its printed values on PARI/GP 2.17.2. A separate modular-symbol calculation with direct point counts gives the corrected gcd and the same exclusions. In residue characteristic 7 the two possible pairs $(a_3,a_5)$ are $(1,4),(4,6)$. The first fails at 3 and the second at 5. At level 1336 the surviving orbit has $a_{41}=0\pmod5$, whereas the family allows 2 or 3. The level-14 positive control survives. The gcd includes the factor $\ell$ at $\ell=p$, and all final residue tests use $\ell\ne p$. [Independent replay and exact output, PR #38](https://github.com/matt-azar/beal-conjecture/pull/38) · [Trace congruences, Siksek, Proposition 5.1](https://samirsiksek.github.io/siksek.github.io/papers/ihpnotes7.pdf)
- For composite $z$, any prime $p\ge5$ dividing $z$ gives the same contradiction upon replacing $c$ by $c^{z/p}$. Thus in this even-$c$, odd-$x$ branch any remaining $z\ge3$ has only prime factors 2 and 3. The review does not settle those exponents or establish publication priority. Bennett–Siksek's complete odd-exponent prime-base table has $q<100$, and their Theorem 4 treats the exponent of $q$ even for odd $q<1000$. [Bennett–Siksek, Theorems 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05553)
