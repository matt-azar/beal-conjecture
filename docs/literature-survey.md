# Literature survey: what could help

The initial survey used abstracts and search excerpts on 2026-11-26. The source check below gives the hypotheses and theorem locators that affect the proposed investigations. The final section records areas still to search.

Goals referred to below: (a) relax the fourth-power condition, (b) one computation covering many first bases, (c) remove $4\mid y$, (d) more fixed bases, (e) anything toward the full conjecture.

## Results that bear on novelty

- In the odd-right-base setting of equation (61), Bennett–Siksek Theorem 12 assumes the right prime exponent is coprime to the class number. The project studies exceptional primes dividing that number. The earlier work on prime $q$ does not directly specialize to composite 2471. [§11, Theorem 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05553)
- Bennett–Siksek cover primes $q<100$ fully and even exponents for $q<1000$. The general odd-exponent families at 167 and 263 lie beyond those ranges.
- Golfieri–Pacetti–Villagra Torcomian cover $d\equiv3\pmod 8$ asymptotically in $p$. The project's bases are outside that too.
- The search did not identify a published theorem containing the claimed full fourth-power families at 167, 263 or 2471. Individual subcases do have published coverage, including even prime-power exponents in Theorem 4 and the signed $(p,p,2)$ branch.

The claimed results use established methods in specific families. Priority and the feasibility of treating arbitrary square summands at the same bases remain open.

## Claims to treat with caution

- **Inter-universal Teichmüller theory.** For a positive primitive solution $a^r+b^s=c^t$ with $r,s,t\ge3$, Zhou I claims $\log(a^r b^s c^t)\le24626$. This bound would give a finite search. Part II claims further exclusions by signature and is marked as an older version. Both use IUT. Scholze and Stix identify a gap in the argument for IUT III, Corollary 3.12, and record Mochizuki's disagreement. The project has not used these bounds. [Zhou I, Theorem 4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14510) · [Zhou II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05448) · [Scholze–Stix, §2](https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/scholze/WhyABCisStillaConjecture.pdf)

## Useful background, low expected payoff

- **The exceptional set for $abc$.** For fixed $0<\lambda<1$, Bernert–Browning–Lichtman–Teräväinen bound the number of positive coprime triples $a+b=c\le X$ with $\operatorname{rad}(abc)<c^\lambda$ by $O_{\lambda,\varepsilon}(X^{3/5+\varepsilon})$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. This permits individual exceptional triples. [Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12234) · [Bernert's earlier bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13364) · [Li's earlier bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02885)
- **Products of valuations.** For positive coprime $a+b=c$, Pasten proves $\prod_{p\mid abc}v_p(abc)\ll_\varepsilon\operatorname{rad}(abc)^{8/3+\varepsilon}$. This controls prime multiplicities and gives no exclusion for the project's fixed bases. [Pasten, Theorem 1.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09251)
- **Campana points and powerful numbers.** Browning–Verzobio bound the number of primitive positive $a+b=c$ of bounded height with $a,b,c$ respectively $p$-, $q$-, $r$-full, meaning each prime multiplicity reaches the specified exponent. Bartsch–Javanpeykar relate nondensity of $S$-integral points on the complement of $x^2y^3=1$ in the affine plane to a case of Campana's orbifold Mordell conjecture. [Browning–Verzobio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24512) · [Bartsch–Javanpeykar, Theorem C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28745)
- **Purely exponential equations with all bases fixed.** Styer bounds the number of positive exponent triples for fixed coprime bases greater than one, each of which is not a perfect power. One range with at most one solution, apart from listed exceptions, is $a,b<3600$ and $c<10^{10}$. The other two bases are unbounded in the project. [Styer, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04428)
- **Asymptotic Fermat over number fields.** Özman–Siksek survey the link with $S$-unit equations. Mocanu proves asymptotic results on $(p,p,2)$ and $(p,p,3)$ over totally real fields under arithmetic and local hypotheses. The imaginary quadratic fields of lead 1 fall outside those hypotheses. [Özman–Siksek](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11289) · [Mocanu, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07873)

## Additions from the full research pass

A longer automated literature pass ran on 2026-11-26 after the survey above. The points below are taken from its report. They add to the source-checked entries and have **not** themselves been checked against the sources by the project, except where stated.

- **Overlap with Bennett–Siksek.** Their Theorem 4 lists every solution of $x^2+q^{2k}=y^n$ for primes $q<1000$, and neither 167 nor 263 occurs. This contains the project's even-$x$ cases at those bases. The source check in PR #9 confirmed this for 167. The authors also say that pushing their odd-exponent theorem beyond $q<100$ is barely tractable with their method, because the exponent bounds from linear forms in logarithms leave a very large sieve.
- **Level of the $\mathbb Q$-curve (lead 1).** From the published level formula, the report computes level $2d^2$ or $2^8d^2$ with trivial character for $d=167$ and $263$: 55,778 or 7,139,584 at 167. According to the report the smaller level arises only when the right base is even. The project excludes that branch by parity or by the class group, so its main branch would sit at the larger level, which is out of reach for a full Hecke computation. The level arithmetic is the report's and the conclusion is the lead's inference. Both are unverified.
- **Lead 3 at $d\equiv7\pmod 8$.** The report's reading is that the asymptotic theorem needs 2 inert, so that there is one prime above 2 and the right base is forced odd. With 2 split both fail. This is an interpretation, not a statement in the paper.
- **Lead 2 in practice.** For $A=167$ the Frey variety for $r=5$ lives over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, where 167 is inert, and for $r=7$ over the cubic field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+$. The report judges full Hilbert newform spaces at the resulting levels to be at or beyond current software, with partial computations modulo $p$ as the realistic option. The authors' code is public. [Repository](https://github.com/akoutsianas/2rp)
- **Smallest open case.** According to the Ratcliffe–Grechuk survey, the smallest signature with all exponents at least 3 that remains open is $(3,5,7)$. Their Table 6.1 of fixed-coefficient results should be checked before any announcement.
- **How often the hard case occurs.** By the Cohen–Lenstra heuristic an odd prime $p$ divides $h(-d)$ with probability about $1/p+1/p^2$. A method that works base by base must therefore handle $p\mid h$ routinely. The twelve-base survey is consistent with this. [Survey results](breadth-survey-results.md)
- **The 263 Mordell curve.** Bennett–Siksek computed the points on $Y^2=X^3\pm q^c$ integral outside $q$ for all $q<100$ with Magma's standard routine. The same routine, or von Känel–Matschke's code, would give an independent check of the denominator argument at 263, and is the tool for the right-exponent-3 case of an arbitrary square.
- **The Lean proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.** The report gives Lean 4.33.1 and Mathlib v4.33.0 under Apache-2.0, against this project's Lean 4.19.0. Whether its modularity and level-lowering statements are general enough for a curve of conductor $32d$ is unknown. It also records that the formalization covers exponents at least 17 directly.
- **An error in the report.** It states that no defective Lucas pair exists at index 13. The pair $(a,b)=(1,-7)$ is one, as the proof and the source check say. [Audit](audit.md)
