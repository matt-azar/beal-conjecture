# Literature survey: what could help

Compiled by the lead on 2026-11-26 from about twenty web searches. **Every entry below was read at the level of an abstract or search excerpt, not the full paper.** Statements of scope are therefore provisional and each needs checking against the source before the project relies on it or cites it (task C4). The survey is broad but not exhaustive. Areas not yet searched are listed at the end.

Goals referred to below: (a) relax the fourth-power condition, (b) one computation covering many first bases, (c) remove $4\mid y$, (d) more fixed bases, (e) anything toward the full conjecture.

## Results that bear on novelty

- The classical primitive-divisor results on $x^2+q^{2k+1}=y^n$ (Arif–Abu Muriefah and successors) assume $q\not\equiv7\pmod8$ and $\gcd(n,3h)=1$. The project's three hard pairs $(d,p)$ sit in the excluded region on both counts.
- Bennett–Siksek cover primes $q<100$ fully and even exponents for $q<1000$. The project's odd-exponent cases at 167 and 263 are outside those theorems.
- Golfieri–Pacetti–Villagra Torcomian cover $d\equiv3\pmod 8$ asymptotically in $p$. The project's bases are outside that too.
- No source found treats $q=167$, $263$ or $D=2471$ specifically. This was not an exhaustive search.

So the project's fixed-base results still appear to be new instances. Leads 1, 3 and 7 show that specialists have the tools to prove stronger statements for the same bases, so the results should be presented as modest.

## Claims to treat with caution

- **Inter-universal Teichmüller theory.** Zhou's preprints ([I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14510), [II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05448)) claim explicit height bounds for every primitive solution with all exponents at least 3. If correct, Beal would reduce to a finite computation. They depend on Mochizuki's theory, which most number theorists do not accept. The project must not build on them.
- **Self-published Beal proofs.** Searches return several non-refereed claimed proofs and claimed counterexamples. None is credible evidence either way.

## Useful background, low expected payoff

- **abc for almost all triples.** Browning–Lichtman–Teräväinen, [exceptional set bound $O(X^{33/50})$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12234), improved by [Bernert](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13364) and [Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02885). Counting results cannot exclude individual solutions.
- **Unconditional partial abc.** Pasten, [Shimura curves and abc](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09251). Far from the strength Beal needs.
- **Campana points and powerful numbers.** [Sums of three powerful numbers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24512), [orbifold Mordell](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28745). Gives the geometric framing (Darmon–Granville) and counting bounds, not exclusions.
- **Purely exponential equations with all bases fixed.** Miyazaki–Pink, Scott–Styer, [at most one solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04428). Needs all three bases fixed. The project fixes one.
- **Asymptotic Fermat over number fields.** Özman–Siksek, [S-unit equations and asymptotic Fermat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11289); Mocanu, [$(p,p,2)$ and $(p,p,3)$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07873). Relevant only if the project moves its Frey curve to a number field, as lead 1 does.

## Not yet searched

Linear forms in logarithms (Matveev, Laurent, Bugeaud–Laurent) and the explicit exponent bounds they would give at $q=167$. The symplectic method and multi-Frey elimination in detail. Uniform large-image results and the status of Serre's uniformity question. Lawrence–Venkatesh. Subspace-theorem results with one fixed base (Corvaja–Zannier). Function-field analogues and arithmetic derivatives. Cohen–Lenstra statistics for how often $p\mid h(-d)$ at $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$. Computational limits for Hilbert modular forms. Recent AI-assisted Diophantine results.
