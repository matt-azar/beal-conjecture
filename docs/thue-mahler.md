# A Thue–Mahler reduction of the two exponent-31 twists

The [modular proof](frey-curve.md) excludes both branches. This reduction gives an independent way to study the associated binary form.

Suppose $2471^x+b^4=c^{31}$ with positive pairwise coprime bases and odd $x\geq3$. The [descent and exponent sieve](descent.md) leaves two cases. Each corresponds to one sign of the **same** irreducible binary form $F$ of degree 31. This gives an effective finite reduction. The integer solutions of the resulting Thue–Mahler equation have yet to be computed here.

| $x$, with $j\geq0$ | Ideal-class index $k$ | Required value of $F(u,v)$ |
|---|---:|---|
| $5+124j$ | 23 | $-2\cdot2471^{2+62j}$ |
| $67+124j$ | 8 | $+2\cdot2471^{33+62j}$ |

## Why both signs use the same form

Write $d=2471$, $s=\sqrt{-d}$, $w=(1+s)/2$, and $R=\mathbb Z[w]$, where $w^2=w-618$. Put $t=(x-1)/2$ and $\gamma=b^2+d^t s$. The conjugate ideals $(\gamma)$ and $(\bar\gamma)$ are coprime and $(\gamma)=I^{31}$. Orient the prime $P$ over 2 by $(\eta)=P^{31}$ with $\eta=(90621+391s)/2=45115+391w$. The index from the sieve means $[I][P]^k=1$.

If $k=8$, set $J=I$ and $\epsilon=+1$. If $k=23$, set $J=\bar I$ and $\epsilon=-1$. Indeed $[\bar I]=[I]^{-1}=[P]^{23}$, so $[\bar I][P]^8=1$. In both cases $JP^8=(\beta)$ with $\beta\in P^8$. Since $R^\times=\{\pm1\}$ and 31 is odd, we can choose the sign of $\beta$ so that

$$
\beta^{31}/\eta^8=b^2+\epsilon d^t s.
$$

## Explicit integral coordinates

Reduction $w\mapsto83\pmod{256}$ respects $w^2-w+618=0$ and kills $\eta$. Its kernel has index 256 and is supported on the chosen $P$: the image of $w$ modulo 2 is 1, whereas the conjugate root is 0. Hence the kernel is $P^8=(256,w-83)$. Adding three times its second basis vector to the first gives the basis $7+3w,w-83$. Write

$$
\beta=(7+3w)u+(w-83)v,\qquad C(u,v)=N(\beta)/256=22u^2+9uv+29v^2 .
$$

Define homogeneous forms $H,F$ by $H(u,v)+w F(u,v)=\beta^{31}/\eta^8$. Each coefficient is integral because all products of 31 basis vectors lie in $P^{248}=(\eta^8)$. The coefficient of $u^{31-i}v^i$ is the corresponding coordinate of

$$
\binom{31}{i}(7+3w)^{31-i}(w-83)^i\bar\eta^8/2^{248}.
$$

A rational prime dividing both $u,v$ would give $(\beta)\subseteq qP^8$, hence $J\subseteq(q)$. Conjugation would also give $\bar J\subseteq(q)$, contradicting coprimality. Thus $\gcd(u,v)=1$. Comparing the two coordinates gives $F=2\epsilon d^t$ and $2H+F=2b^2$. Taking norms gives $c=C(u,v)>0$.

Conversely, take any primitive pair $(u,v)$ with $F$ as in a row of the table. Require $H+F/2$ to be the square of a positive integer $b$, and $d,b,C(u,v)$ to be pairwise coprime. These conditions suffice. The norm identity
$N(H+wF)=H^2+HF+618F^2=C(u,v)^{31}$ then reads $b^4+d^{2t+1}=C(u,v)^{31}$. This converse recovers $c$ without extracting a large integer root.

## Why an irreducible Thue–Mahler solver applies

Over $K=\mathbb Q(s)$, the coordinate $F$ is the difference between $\beta^{31}/\eta^8$ and its conjugate, divided by $s$. In projective coordinates the equation $F=0$ therefore becomes

$$
(\beta/\bar\beta)^{31}=(\eta/\bar\eta)^8.
$$

The two linear forms $\beta,\bar\beta$ are independent because their coefficient determinant is a nonzero multiple of $256s$. The change of projective coordinates is invertible. The right side has no 31st root in $K$. Such a root would generate the fractional ideal $(P/\bar P)^8$, whose class $[P]^{16}$ has order 31. If $T^{31}-a$ for $a=(\eta/\bar\eta)^8$ had a proper irreducible factor over $K$ of degree $m<31$, a root $\theta$ of that factor would satisfy $N_{K(\theta)/K}(\theta)^{31}=a^m$. Bézout's identity with $m,31$ would then make $a$ a 31st power in $K$. Thus $F$ is irreducible over $K$ and over $\mathbb Q$. Its leading coefficient is nonzero, equal to $-12305514202952265789$.

It now suffices to solve $F(u,v)=\pm 2\cdot7^e353^f$ with $e,f\geq0$ and $\gcd(u,v)=1$, and retain the matching values $e=f=t$ and the square condition. Irreducible Thue–Mahler equations have finitely many primitive solutions and algorithms to determine them. Gherga and Siksek's method uses the field generated by one root of $F(T,1)$. Their Magma implementation uses $\gcd(F(1,0),v)=1$. Here this is automatic because $\gcd(F(1,0),2d)=1$, and a prime dividing both $F(1,0)$ and $v$ would divide $F(u,v)$. A practical degree-31 computation still needs certified number-field data, an explicit exponent bound and a complete enumeration or exclusion. An unbounded pair search supplies none of those steps. [Gherga and Siksek, *Algebra & Number Theory* 19 (2025), 667–714](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2025.19.667)

## The local lines and an unconditional lower bound for $c$

Put $f(T)=F(T,1)$. Reduction of the integral coefficients gives the following complete list of affine roots. Since neither prime divides $F(1,0)$, a primitive solution must have $v$ a unit at both primes.

| $q$ | Roots of $f$ modulo $q$ | Root with $C(T,1)$ a unit | $f'$ at that root modulo $q$ |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 7 | 0, 6 | 0 | 3 |
| 353 | 72, 119 | 119 | 276 |

Each unit-norm root has a unique Hensel lift $\rho_q\in\mathbb Z_q$. Factoring $f(T)=(T-\rho_q)g_q(T)$ over $\mathbb Z_q$ shows that $g_q(u/v)$ is a unit on that residue class. The two valuations of $F$ in the table at the top of the note are precisely $t$, so $v_q(u/v-\rho_q)=t$ at both primes.

One further line is forced modulo 683. For the chosen sign in each branch, test the two reductions of $\eta^8(b^2+\epsilon d^t s)$ at $s=332,-332$ as 31st powers. For the representatives $x=5,67$, the complete surviving residues of $b$ are respectively $\pm26,\pm298$. In both cases $b^2+\epsilon d^t s$ vanishes at $s=332$. Increasing $x$ by 124 scales the expression by a unit after rescaling $b$, preserving the same vanishing component. Thus $683\mid c$ and $\beta$ vanishes at $w=(1+332)/2\equiv508\pmod{683}$. Its linear expression in $u,v$ then forces $u\equiv163v\pmod{683}$. This distinguishes between the two roots 116 and 163 of $C(T,1)$ modulo 683.

Combining the three lines by the Chinese remainder theorem gives a single relation $u\equiv r_t v\pmod{683d^t}$, hence a rank-two lattice with basis $(683d^t,0),(r_t,1)$. Every actual pair belongs to this lattice. At the minimum $t$ for each branch, Gauss reduction with the positive norm $C$ gives the following lower bounds. Larger $t$ gives a sublattice by compatibility of the Hensel lifts.

| $x$ | Minimum $t$ | Lower bound on $c$ throughout the branch |
|---|---:|---:|
| $5+124j$ | 2 | $61{,}556{,}730{,}755$ |
| $67+124j$ | 33 | $>3\cdot10^{115}$ |

The verifier checks the lattice determinant and both final basis vectors. For a reduced basis $v_1,v_2$, write $A=C(v_1)$, $D=C(v_2)$, $L=C(v_1+v_2)-A-D$. The checks give $A\leq D$ and $|L|\leq A$. Hence $C(mv_1+nv_2)\geq A(m^2-|mn|+n^2)\geq A$ for every nonzero integer pair. At $t=2$, one shortest vector is $(-31458,42241)$. This calculation bounds the height from below. It leaves all larger heights subject to the remaining form equation.

Run `python3 scripts/check_residual_form.py --coefficients`. The program computes all coefficients with exact integer arithmetic and checks five primitive input pairs against the defining identity in $R$ and the norm formula. For example $(u,v)=(0,1)$ gives $C=29$, $H=11691118074963636910747$ and $F=-1818639441937051098764$. These pairs test the construction. The printed arrays are ordered from the coefficient of $u^{31}$ to that of $v^{31}$. [Verifier](../scripts/check_residual_form.py)
