# Independent review of the square-summand claim at 2471

I found no gap in the exclusion of positive primitive solutions of $2471^x+U^2=c^{31}$. This review covers PR #18 at `e876b5d`. It corrects four inaccuracies in the numerical description and independently verifies the computations used by the proof. External specialist review, priority in the literature and formalization of the complete theorem remain open.

## Proof from an integer solution

| Case | Check |
|---|---|
| $x$ even | Set $V=2471^{x/2}$. Coprimality and parity make $U+iV$ and its conjugate coprime in the Gaussian integers. A 31st root $u+iv$ has coprime coordinates and odd norm $c$. Both coordinates are nonzero, and equality $u=\pm v$ would give even norm. Thus its ratio with its conjugate is not a root of unity, and $2u$ and $c$ are coprime. The primitive divisor theorem applies at index 31. Its prime $q$ divides $V$, so $q=7$ or $353$. Modulo a prime above $q$, the ratio has order 31 by primitivity. In the split case its order divides $q-1$, and in the inert case conjugation is Frobenius, so its order divides $q+1$. Neither prime meets the condition. [Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier, Theorem 1.4 in the authors’ report](https://scispace.com/pdf/existence-of-primitive-divisors-of-lucas-and-lehmer-numbers-42oopgc0q5.pdf) |
| $x$ odd, $c$ even | The reduced forms give class number 62. The odd trace and norm $2^{31}$ of $\eta$ give $(\eta)=P^{31}$ at one prime above 2. A norm-two element would require $u^2+2471v^2=8$, so both primes above 2 have order 31. Exactly one of them divides $\delta$ since its norm is even and its trace is odd. All odd valuations in $(\delta)$ are multiples of 31. Thus $2[Q]=31[J]$: the left side has order 31 and the right side has order dividing 2. This is the contradiction. [Class data](descent.md) |
| $x,c$ odd, $31\mid x$ | In $c^{31}+(-2471^{x/31})^{31}=U^2$ the signed bases are coprime, all entries are nonzero, and their product has absolute value greater than one. Darmon–Merel's signed primitive $(n,n,2)$ theorem applies for $n=31$. [Main Theorem, part 2](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/darmon/pub/Articles/Research/18.Merel/paper.pdf) |
| $x,c$ odd, $31\nmid x$ | The conjugate principal ideals are coprime. The 31-torsion is generated by $[P]$, giving a single global $k$ for the local checks. The numerical test includes $k=0$. At 2, $v_2(\Delta)=6$ implies minimality. After translation $8\mid a_6'$ and $b'_8\equiv4\pmod8$ give type III and conductor exponent 5. At $q\mid 2471c$ the invariant $c_4$ is a unit. The discriminant valuations are $x$ at 7 and 353 and $62v_q(c)$ at $q\mid c$, including $q=31$ if relevant. Mazur excludes a rational 31-isogeny. The lowered level is $32\cdot2471=79072$. Siksek's unsigned trace congruence suffices at the primes dividing $c$. [Cremona, §3.2](https://johncremona.github.io/book/fulltext/chapter3.pdf) · [Mazur, Theorem 1](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~goldfeld/Mazur-Goldfeld1978.pdf) · [Siksek, Theorem 3 and Proposition 5.1](https://samirsiksek.github.io/siksek.github.io/papers/ihpnotes7.pdf) |

## Corrections to the original note

- The signed first stage uses 26 primes including 3. The unsigned first stage uses 24 including 3.
- $S_{17}$ contains $-8$. For $x=3$ and $U\equiv0\pmod{17}$ the local equation has $c\equiv10$ and the fiber $Y^2=X^3+12X$ has 26 points. The emitted filter includes residue 23 modulo 31.
- The maximum signed class-set sizes at 61, 683 and 1117 are 4, 6 and 7. At $(t,k)=(1,18)$ the last set is $\{2,4,5,13,18,26,27\}$. With both multiplicative signs allowed the maximum there is 8. The program retained every residue.
- The fourth-power control needs both lifts of $x$ modulo 124. Its four surviving $(x,k)$ are $(5,23),(31,0),(67,8),(93,0)$. Increasing $x$ by 62 scales $U$ by $2471^{31}$, which can change its square class. The arbitrary-$U$ proof allows every $U$ and all 961 classes modulo $(31,31)$ in $(t,k)$.

## Independent computation

The review script enumerates the 31st powers in the residue algebra first, multiplies by $\eta^{-k}$, then reads the real coefficient $U$ and the coefficient $a$ of $s$. It compares $a$ with every power $2471^e$, keeping $e\bmod31$. This reverses the search in the original verifier and includes zero in either split component. All 2,883 signed sets and all 2,883 unsigned sets agree. The auxiliary descent sieve with modulus 961 and the 22 split primes congruent to 1 modulo 31 through 11719 leaves 81 classes before the Hecke test.

Starting from full relative modular symbols in eclib gives 21 retained dimensions in each complex-conjugation sign. The level-32 CM control has multiplicity eight and agrees with point counts at all fourteen primes.
